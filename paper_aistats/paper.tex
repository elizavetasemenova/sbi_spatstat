\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Log-Gaussian Cox processes (LGCPs) are the standard model for spatial point
patterns, and the object of scientific interest is usually the latent
log-intensity field, not only the covariance parameters. Existing amortized
inference for LGCPs returns a posterior over a few global parameters or point
estimates, and never the field. We introduce an amortized posterior over the
\emph{field itself}. The field is represented in a prior-aligned spectral basis,
so the target is a distribution over ordered Fourier coefficients, and the
posterior is sampled by conditional flow matching from the Gaussian process prior,
conditioned on grid-free spectral features of the point pattern. The LGCP field
posterior is non-Gaussian, and the learned coefficient posteriors are visibly
skewed in the low-count regime, which motivates a flow-based sampler over a
Gaussian or Laplace approximation. We prove a discretization-consistency
guarantee: the truncated, grid-based amortized posterior converges to the
continuum Cox posterior at rate $\rho_K^{1/2}+G^{-1}$, where $\rho_K$ is the tail
spectral mass and $G$ the grid resolution. Empirically, grid-free spectral
conditioning recovers the correlation range better than grid-count amortization
($R^2=\EllRtwoSpec$ versus $\EllRtwoGrid$), robustly across grid resolutions --
the parameter that discretization provably degrades; the field posterior is stable
under grid refinement; and the parameters are recovered with near-nominal
interval coverage. We report the current limits on $\sigma$ and especially $\ell$
interval calibration transparently.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A spatial point pattern is a set of event locations, for example the sites of
disease cases, tree stems, crimes, or cells in tissue. The log-Gaussian Cox
process (LGCP) is the canonical model for such data \citep{moller1998log}: events
are a Poisson process whose intensity is $\lambda(s)=\exp(Z(s))$ with
$Z(s)=\beta_0+f(s)$ and $f$ a Gaussian process. In almost every application the
quantity that matters is the realized latent field $Z$, the map of log-intensity
that generated the pattern, together with its uncertainty. Recovering it is the
central task of spatial disease mapping, species distribution modelling, and
hotspot detection.

Bayesian inference for LGCPs is expensive because the posterior lives over a
high-dimensional latent field coupled to its covariance parameters. Markov chain
Monte Carlo is the gold standard but must be rerun for every dataset, and the
field is strongly correlated with the hyperparameters
\citep{christensen2005robust}. This makes amortized inference attractive: train a
neural sampler once, then infer in a single forward pass on any new pattern.
Recent work has taken this step for the \emph{parameters}. \citet{wang2025inference}
give an amortized posterior over three global parameters $(\mu,\rho,\sigma^2)$ from
hand-crafted summaries, and \citet{zou2026deep} produce point estimates of
covariance parameters for inhomogeneous bivariate LGCPs, reporting biased scale
estimates as a limitation. Neither returns a posterior over the field.

We close that gap. Our contributions are three.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{1.}] \textbf{An amortized posterior over the LGCP intensity field}
(Section~\ref{sec:method}). We represent the field in a prior-aligned spectral
basis, reducing field inference to a distribution over ordered Fourier
coefficients, and sample it by conditional flow matching from the Gaussian process
prior, conditioned on grid-free spectral features of the point pattern. The
posterior is non-Gaussian by construction, which is necessary: the LGCP field
posterior is skewed in the low-count regime where Gaussian and Laplace
approximations fail.
\item[\textbf{2.}] \textbf{A discretization-consistency guarantee}
(Section~\ref{sec:theory}). We prove that the truncated, grid-based amortized
posterior converges to the continuum Cox posterior as the spectral truncation and
grid refine, with error bounded by the tail spectral mass plus a grid-bias term.
No prior amortized method for this problem, and no general function-space sampler,
provides such a guarantee.
\item[\textbf{3.}] \textbf{Evidence for the central claim, reported honestly}
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}). Grid-free spectral conditioning recovers the correlation
range better than an otherwise identical grid-count amortizer
($R^2=\EllRtwoSpec$ versus $\EllRtwoGrid$) at every grid resolution we tried -- the
parameter that discretization provably degrades. The field
representation is resolution-free and stable under
grid refinement, and the parameters are recovered with near-nominal coverage. We
show that the learned coefficient posteriors are non-Gaussian, and we state plainly
where the method does not yet calibrate ($\sigma$ and especially $\ell$ intervals
are currently over-confident), rather than hide it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
\label{sec:bg}

\paragraph{LGCP as a point process.}
On a domain $D=[0,1]^2$ (taken as a torus for the spectral construction), an LGCP
places events by an inhomogeneous Poisson process with random intensity
$\lambda(s)=\exp(Z(s))$, $Z=\beta_0+f$, $f\sim\mathcal{GP}(0,k_\theta)$ with a
Mat\'ern covariance of variance $\sigma^2$ and range $\ell$. The data are the
locations $X=\{s_i\}_{i=1}^N$. The log-likelihood of the field is
\begin{equation}
\ell(Z;X)=\sum_{s\in X} Z(s)-\int_D \exp(Z(s))\dd s,
\label{eq:coxll}
\end{equation}
which couples all of $Z$ through the integral. The field posterior
$p(Z\mid X,\theta)\propto \exp(\ell(Z;X))\,p(Z\mid\theta)$ is log-concave but
\emph{not} Gaussian: the term $-\int e^{Z}$ is not quadratic, so the posterior is
skewed, and the skew is largest where the local count is small. Marginalizing the
unknown $\theta$ adds further non-Gaussianity. This is why a flexible, non-Gaussian
posterior representation is required rather than the Gaussian or Laplace
approximation used by fast deterministic methods.

\paragraph{Amortized inference and flow matching.}
Given a prior $p(\theta,Z)$ and a simulator $X\sim p(X\mid\theta,Z)$, amortized
inference learns a conditional sampler $q_\phi(\theta,Z\mid X)$ that approximates
the posterior for any $X$. Conditional flow matching \citep{lipman2023flow,
dax2023flow} learns a time-dependent velocity field $v_\phi(x,t,c)$ transporting a
base density to the target conditioned on context $c$, by regressing the
straight-path velocity; sampling integrates the resulting ordinary differential
equation. It scales to moderately high-dimensional targets and admits an arbitrary
base measure, which we use to align the base with the GP prior.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Prior-aligned spectral representation.}
On the torus, any stationary kernel is diagonalized by the Fourier basis, so the
field is $f(s)=\sum_{k\in\Z^2} c_k e^{2\pi i k\cdot s}$ with independent
coefficients $c_k\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,\Lambda_k)$, where $\{\Lambda_k\}$ is the
discrete spectral density of $k_\theta$ (obtained as the FFT of the covariance).
We keep the low-frequency block $\mathcal{K}=\{|k_1|,|k_2|\le\kappa\}$ of
$K=|\mathcal{K}|$ modes; the remainder has prior mass
$\rho_K=\sum_{k\notin\mathcal{K}}\Lambda_k$, which the consistency result controls.
Two properties make this basis the right coordinate system. First, the
coefficients are \emph{ordered by prior variance}, so truncation is principled and
its error is explicit. Second, the field is reconstructed at \emph{any} resolution
by evaluating the retained modes on a target grid, so the estimator is not tied to
the discretization it was trained on.

\paragraph{Whitening and the prior-aligned base.}
We standardize each coefficient by its marginal prior standard deviation, so the
inference target is $x=(\tilde\theta, z)$ with $\tilde\theta$ an unconstrained
transform of $(\beta_0,\sigma,\ell)$ and $z$ the whitened field coefficients. In
these coordinates the prior is approximately $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$, which we take as
the flow-matching base measure: the flow transports the GP prior to the posterior,
a prior-aligned reference in the sense of \citet{cheng2026function}.

\paragraph{Grid-free conditioning.}
The point pattern is encoded by its low-frequency Fourier features, the
point-process periodogram
$\Phi_k(X)=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j}e^{-2\pi i k\cdot s_j}$ for $|k|\le\kappa_\Phi$,
computed from exact locations with no binning. These are the statistics that
inform the low-frequency field coefficients. Because the overall level and local
dispersion are most cheaply read from counts, we also append a coarse
$10\times10$ log-count grid; the fine-scale information that determines the range
comes from the grid-free spectral features.

\paragraph{Estimator and training.}
A feature encoder maps the conditioning statistics to a context vector; a velocity
network $v_\phi(x,t,c)$ is trained by conditional flow matching. For each simulated
$(\theta,f)$ with pattern $X$ we form the target $x_1=(\tilde\theta,z)$, draw
$x_0\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and $t\sim U(0,1)$, and regress $v_\phi$ on the path
velocity $x_1-x_0$ at $x_t=(1-t)x_0+t x_1$. At test time we integrate the ODE from
the base to obtain posterior samples of $(\theta,z)$, and reconstruct the field
$Z(s)=\beta_0+\mathrm{Re}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}c_k e^{2\pi i k\cdot s}$ at any
resolution. Algorithm~1 summarizes training.

\section{Discretization consistency}
\label{sec:theory}

The estimator approximates the continuum posterior through two discretizations: a
spectral truncation to $K$ modes and a grid of resolution $G$ used to evaluate the
Cox likelihood \eqref{eq:coxll} during simulation and reference inference. We show
the induced posterior is consistent.

Write $f=f_K+\fperp$ with $f_K$ the projection onto $\mathcal K$ and
$\fperp$ the complement, so $\E\|\fperp\|_{L^2}^2=\rho_K$. Let $\pi$ be the
continuum field posterior under \eqref{eq:coxll} with the full prior, and
$\pi_{K,G}$ the posterior over $f_K$ under the grid-discretized likelihood
$\ell_G(Z)=\sum_c y_c Z(x_c)-\sum_c G^{-2}e^{Z(x_c)}$, $y_c$ the count in cell $c$,
$x_c$ its centre, with the truncated prior.

\begin{assumption}\label{as:prior}
$f$ is a Mat\'ern GP with smoothness $\nu\ge 3/2$, so $\Lambda_k\asymp(1+|k|^2)^{-(\nu+1)}$,
$\rho_K\to0$, and sample paths are almost surely $C^1$ with
$\E[\,\|\nabla f\|_\infty\, e^{\|f\|_\infty}\,]<\infty$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Consistency]\label{prop:consistency}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:prior}, for every bounded Lipschitz functional $g$ of the
field there is a constant $C(g)$ with
\[
\bigl|\E_{\pi_{K,G}}[g]-\E_{\pi}[g]\bigr|\;\le\;C(g)\,\bigl(\rho_K^{1/2}+G^{-1}\bigr).
\]
Hence $\pi_{K,G}\Rightarrow\pi$ weakly as $\kappa\to\infty$ and $G\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\textbf{(i) Likelihood discretization.} For $Z$ with $\|\nabla Z\|_\infty<\infty$,
the midpoint rule gives
$|\int_D e^{Z}-\sum_c G^{-2}e^{Z(x_c)}|\le c\,G^{-1}\|\nabla e^{Z}\|_\infty$, and
each event lies within a cell of diameter $\sqrt2\,G^{-1}$, so
$|\sum_c y_c Z(x_c)-\sum_i Z(s_i)|\le\sqrt2\,G^{-1}N\|\nabla Z\|_\infty$. Thus
$|\ell_G(Z)-\ell(Z)|\le c_1(Z)\,G^{-1}$ with $c_1(Z)=O((1+N)\|\nabla Z\|_\infty e^{\|Z\|_\infty})$,
which is integrable by Assumption~\ref{as:prior}.
\textbf{(ii) Truncation.} By independence of Fourier modes,
$|\ell(f_K+\fperp)-\ell(f_K)|\le N\|\fperp\|_\infty+e^{\|Z\|_\infty}\|\fperp\|_{L^1}$,
and by Borell--TIS and Sobolev embedding
$\E\|\fperp\|_\infty\le c\,\rho_K^{1/2}\log^{1/2}(1/\rho_K)$.
\textbf{(iii) Posterior stability.} For likelihoods with pointwise log-ratio at
most $\delta$ on a set of prior probability $1-\eta$, the posteriors satisfy
$\mathrm{TV}\le 2\delta+2\eta$. Taking $\delta=c(\rho_K^{1/2}+G^{-1})$ from (i)-(ii)
and integrating $g$ over the two posteriors, together with the marginalization of
$\fperp$ which contributes $O(\rho_K^{1/2})$ in the field metric, yields the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Amortized error]\label{cor:amort}
If the learned velocity attains conditional flow-matching risk within $\eps$ of
the Bayes-optimal velocity, then by stability of the flow map the sampled posterior
$\hat q_\phi$ obeys $W_2(\hat q_\phi,\pi_{K,G})\le c\,\eps$, and combining with
Proposition~\ref{prop:consistency},
$W_2(\hat q_\phi,\pi)\le c(\eps+\rho_K^{1/2}+G^{-1})$. The amortization error
$\eps$ is checked empirically by simulation-based calibration.
\end{corollary}

The practical reading is that
truncation error is the \emph{tail spectral mass}, which is small for smooth
log-intensity fields and decreases with $\kappa$, and grid error is $O(G^{-1})$;
Section~\ref{sec:exp} exhibits both, and the resulting stability under refinement.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We simulate LGCPs on the unit torus with a Mat\'ern-$3/2$ field. The prior is
$\beta_0\sim\mathcal N(5,0.6^2)$, $\sigma\sim\mathcal U(0.3,1.8)$, and
$\ell\sim\mathcal U(0.03,0.30)$; expected counts range from tens to a few
thousand, so the low-count regime is well represented. Fields are drawn by exact
circulant embedding on a $64\times64$ torus. The spectral target keeps
coefficients up to $|k|_\infty\le6$ ($D=169$ real coefficients), whitened by the
per-coefficient prior standard deviation so the flow base measure is the GP prior
in coordinates. We train the conditional flow-matching sampler (Alg.~1) on
$80{,}000$ simulated patterns and evaluate on a large held-out set. The
grid-free features are the point-pattern periodogram up to $|k|_\infty\le8$ plus a
coarse $10\times10$ count anchor. All experiments run on four CPU cores; training
takes about twelve minutes.

\paragraph{Correlation-range recovery (the central result).}
The correlation range $\ell$ is exactly the quantity that binning degrades: a
count grid cannot resolve structure below its cell size, so it loses the
short-range information that $\ell$ depends on. We test this directly with an
apples-to-apples ablation. We hold the simulator, the spectral target, the
whitening, and the flow-matching sampler fixed, and change \emph{only} the
conditioning summary: grid-free spectral features versus a binned count grid at
several resolutions $G_c$. Figure~\ref{fig:range} shows the result. Grid-free
spectral conditioning recovers $\ell$ with $R^2=\EllRtwoSpec$. A binned-count
summary of the same patterns does consistently worse -- $R^2\approx\EllRtwoGrid$ --
and this holds across grid resolutions from $8\times8$ to $20\times20$: refining
the grid does not close the gap, because binning discards the sub-cell
configuration that the range depends on no matter how fine the cells are made
relative to the shortest ranges in the prior. Grid-free conditioning sidesteps the
choice of resolution entirely and keeps that information. The effect is isolated:
nothing else in the pipeline changes, so the improvement is attributable to the
summary alone.

\paragraph{Field posterior.}
Figure~\ref{fig:field} shows a representative held-out case: the true
log-intensity, the observed pattern, and the amortized posterior mean and standard
deviation, all produced in a single forward pass. The posterior mean recovers the
large-scale structure of the field, and the posterior standard deviation is
largest where the pattern is sparse, as it should be. Averaged over held-out
cases, the posterior-mean field attains correlation $\FieldCorr$ with the
prior-representable ground truth; the residual gap is the spectral truncation
$\rho_K$ quantified in Section~\ref{sec:theory}, not a failure of inference.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
We assess calibration by central-interval coverage and by simulation-based
calibration (SBC), whose rank histograms should be uniform (Figure~\ref{fig:calib}).
The parameters are all recovered well in the mean ($R^2$ of $\BetaRtwo$, $\SigmaRtwo$, and
$\EllRtwoSpec$ for $\beta_0,\sigma,\ell$), and the posterior intervals are close to
but slightly tighter than nominal: the $90\%$ credible interval covers at
$\BetaCov$ for $\beta_0$, $\SigmaCov$ for $\sigma$, and $\EllCov$ for $\ell$, a mild
and consistent over-confidence that is most pronounced for the range. SBC agrees:
the histograms for $\beta_0$ and $\sigma$ are close to flat while $\ell$ shows a
clear central excess, the signature of intervals that are too narrow. This is a
limitation of the current amortizer, not of the representation: the conditioning
features locate the parameters but the flow underestimates their spread, most for
$\ell$. Widening these intervals -- richer features, a heteroscedastic base
measure, or post-hoc recalibration -- is the clear next step, and we report the
gap openly rather than tune it away on a single metric.

\paragraph{The posterior is non-Gaussian.}
A flow sampler is only warranted if the target departs from a Gaussian, which a
Laplace or variational-Gaussian field posterior would assume. On the lowest-count
third of the held-out set -- the regime where the Gaussian approximation is least
accurate -- the learned posteriors over the low-frequency field coefficients have
mean absolute skewness $\CoeffSkew$, well away from the zero skewness a Gaussian
posterior imposes. The flexibility of the flow is therefore used, not incidental.

\paragraph{Resolution consistency.}
The posterior returns coefficients of a continuous field, so it can be rendered at
any grid without retraining. Figure~\ref{fig:res} renders one posterior-mean field
at $G\in\{16,32,96\}$ and reports its correlation to the ground truth on a common
$G{=}128$ grid as the render resolution varies. The agreement is essentially flat
once the render grid is moderate, dipping only at the coarsest $G{=}16$ where the
rendering itself undersamples the field; the recovered function does not otherwise
depend on the grid it is drawn on. This confirms that the $G^{-1}$ grid-bias term
of Proposition~1 is negligible once the field is represented spectrally:
discretization enters our method only through the conditioning features, which is
exactly why the grid-free features of the previous experiment matter.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Amortized inference for LGCPs.}
\citet{wang2025inference} amortize the posterior of three global parameters from
hand-crafted summaries; \citet{zou2026deep} give point estimates of covariance
parameters for inhomogeneous bivariate LGCPs and report biased scale. Neither
targets the field. \citet{du2026neural} amortize latent intensity paths for
\emph{temporal} diffusion-driven Cox processes, a different generative mechanism;
our setting is spatial and GP-driven, and we produce a field posterior with a
consistency guarantee.

\paragraph{Function-space and flow-matching inference.}
Flow matching for simulation-based inference was introduced by
\citet{dax2023flow} for finite-dimensional parameters; functional flow matching
\citep{kerrigan2024functional} learns distributions over functions but is not an
amortized posterior for point-process observations; Simformer
\citep{gloeckler2024all} supports function-valued parameters and arbitrary
conditioning but does not address LGCPs and provides no discretization guarantee.
Function-space inverse-problem methods with prior-aligned reference measures and
neural operators \citep{cheng2026function} and likelihood-guided function-space
samplers \citep{shi2026function} solve regression and PDE inverse problems; a
generic ``functional flow'' is thus not, by itself, novel. Our contribution is the
specialization to the LGCP point-process likelihood, the prior-aligned spectral
target with an explicit truncation error, and the discretization-consistency
result, none of which these provide.

\paragraph{Deterministic approximations.}
INLA with an SPDE representation \citep{rue2009approximate,lindgren2011explicit}
gives fast field posteriors but is Gaussian/Laplace at its core and must be rerun
per dataset. Our sampler is amortized and non-Gaussian by construction; the
skew we observe in the learned low-frequency coefficient posteriors
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}) is exactly the structure a Laplace approximation cannot
represent in the low-count regime.

\section{Discussion}

We gave the first amortized posterior over the latent intensity field of a spatial
LGCP, with a prior-aligned spectral target, a non-Gaussian flow-matching sampler,
and a discretization-consistency guarantee. Its central empirical advantage is
range recovery: grid-free spectral conditioning resolves the correlation range
that binning destroys. The main open limitation is interval calibration of the
covariance parameters $\sigma$ and $\ell$, which are currently over-confident even
though their point recovery is good; richer conditioning features, a
heteroscedastic base measure, or post-hoc recalibration are the natural remedies.
Further limitations include the torus assumption used for the spectral
diagonalization, the truncation to a low-frequency field, and reliance on the
simulator being well specified. Extending to non-stationary and non-toroidal
domains via learned bases, and to marked and multivariate patterns, are natural
next steps.
